    How GridAnonymizer works

    1. Drop your model files on the Anonymize tab. Add the files that belong together in one go, such as every profile of a CGMES set, several cases of one PSS®E system, or the CSVs that go with them, so they get the same pseudonyms.
    2. Check the format shown next to each file, and pick a profile. The Standard profile suits most sharing.
    3. Press Anonymize, then read the verdict. Green means the audit found nothing identifying in the output. Red means don't share that file; open Details to see what was found.
    4. Download the files, and the mapping if you'll need to read the vendor's results later.

    What changes and what stays

    Replaced: names, ids, mRIDs, descriptions, comments, model headers, PSS®E case titles, coordinates and addresses, and optionally bus numbers. Kept exactly: topology, impedances, ratings, switch states, kV levels and shared labels such as MW or High1.

    Salt or mapping?

    Both make pseudonyms repeatable. The salt is a secret word: the same salt and the same files give the same output. The mapping is the full list of original-to-pseudonym pairs. Use it to restore results, or to continue with the next revision of a model. Keep both private; anyone holding the mapping can undo everything.

    Reading results that come back

    On the Restore tab, drop the files the vendor sent (CSV results, contingency lists, even the anonymized model) and choose the mapping. Names, ids and bus numbers are translated back to the originals.

    If the audit fails

    Usually a kept comment, a case title or your company's name inside free text. Open Details on the red file, then on the Settings tab either turn off Keep comments and Keep case titles, or add the word to Terms that must not survive, and run again.

    Privacy

    Everything runs on this computer. The page is not allowed to make network requests, so your files cannot be uploaded.
